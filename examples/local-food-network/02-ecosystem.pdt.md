# Ecosystem

Who takes part in the local food ecosystem, and what drives each of them.

## Harvest Commons Cooperative

The cooperative runs the platform on behalf of its members. It is owned by the farms, employs
two people and is governed by a monthly members' assembly.

```pdt
:::entity
id: e-harvest-coop
title: Harvest Commons Cooperative
role: shaper
context:
  - Two staff, owned by 14 member farms
  - Grew out of a shared market stall
pressures:
  - Must break even on a thin commission
  - Members expect fair, transparent rules
gains:
  - A sustainable 8 % commission base
  - A stronger regional food system
seeks:
  - Members who feel it is their platform
resources:
  - Customer base from the Saturday market
  - Trust of the farming community
:::
```

## Small-Scale Farmers

Family farms of two to twenty hectares within 60 km of the city. They grow vegetables, fruit and
herbs, sell at markets and to a few loyal shops, and throw away up to a fifth of what they harvest.

```pdt
:::entity
id: e-farmers
title: Small-Scale Farmers
role: peer-producer
context:
  - 2–20 ha, mixed vegetables and fruit
  - Sell at markets and to a few shops
pressures:
  - Volatile demand, up to 20 % waste
  - Rising fuel and labour costs
  - No time for marketing
gains:
  - Predictable income before the season starts
  - Less driving, less waste
seeks:
  - Selling as simple as loading a crate
resources:
  - Fresh seasonal produce
  - Deep knowledge of soil and seasons
  - Farm land for visits
:::
```

## Food Artisans

Bakers, cheesemakers and preservers who process local produce. They add variety to every box and
buy the surplus that would otherwise be wasted.

```pdt
:::entity
id: e-food-artisans
title: Food Artisans
role: peer-producer
context:
  - Micro-bakeries, dairies, preserve makers
pressures:
  - Hard to reach customers beyond their shop
gains:
  - Steady add-on sales
  - Access to farm surplus at fair prices
seeks:
  - A shelf in every harvest box
resources:
  - Bread, cheese, preserves
  - Capacity to process surplus
:::
```

## Neighbourhood Households

Families and flat shares who care about where food comes from but shop at the supermarket because
it is convenient. They are the bulk of the demand.

```pdt
:::entity
id: e-households
title: Neighbourhood Households
role: peer-consumer
context:
  - Urban families and flat shares
  - Cook at home three to five times a week
pressures:
  - Little time for shopping
  - Food prices rising faster than income
gains:
  - Fresh food with a face behind it
  - A fair price without going to the market
seeks:
  - Pick-up as easy as the corner shop
  - Recipes for what is in the box
resources:
  - Weekly subscription commitment
  - Word of mouth in the neighbourhood
:::
```

## Independent Restaurants

Chef-owned restaurants that write their menu around the season. They buy in larger quantities and
can commit to volumes months ahead.

```pdt
:::entity
id: e-restaurants
title: Independent Restaurants
role: peer-consumer
context:
  - Chef-owned, 30–80 covers
pressures:
  - Wholesalers offer little local produce
  - Menus depend on unreliable supply
gains:
  - Guaranteed local produce for signature dishes
  - A story guests remember
seeks:
  - A direct line to the farmer
resources:
  - Bulk demand planned months ahead
  - Culinary reputation that promotes farms
:::
```

## City Food Council

The council advises the city on its food strategy, including the target of 30 % regional food in
public canteens by 2030.

```pdt
:::entity
id: e-city
title: City Food Council
role: stakeholder
context:
  - Advisory board to the city
pressures:
  - 30 % regional food target by 2030
gains:
  - Evidence that local sourcing works
seeks:
  - Data on food miles and local share
resources:
  - Public canteen demand
  - Access to city-owned hub locations
:::
```
